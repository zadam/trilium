Meeting transcription is in beta

Meeting transcription is a new feature and is still in beta. It has not been tested on every operating system, audio setup and meeting application, so it might fail to capture the audio, miss part of a meeting or behave differently from what this page describes. Before relying on it for an important meeting, try it on a short call first.

If something does not work, please report the issue, mentioning your operating system and the meeting application you used. The Keep recordings for troubleshooting option described below keeps what is needed to reproduce a problem.

Meeting transcription turns a meeting into text while it happens: Trilium listens to your microphone and to what the other participants say through your speakers, and writes down who said what in a new note. Recognition runs entirely on your device, so no audio leaves it.

Meeting transcription is available only in the desktop application. It is not available when Trilium is accessed through a browser, nor in the mobile application.

Enabling meeting transcription

Meeting transcription is off by default. To turn it on, go to Options → Transcription and turn on Meeting transcription. This adds the Transcribe meeting button to the Launch Bar, marked with a Beta badge.

The speech recognition models are not part of Trilium. The first time you transcribe a meeting, Trilium asks to download them; see Downloaded resources for the sizes and how to manage them. Each device downloads its own copy.

Transcribing a meeting

  1. Press the Transcribe meeting button in the Launch Bar.
  2. If the models are not downloaded yet, confirm the download and wait for it to finish.
  3. Trilium creates a text note titled with the current date and time (for example Meeting 2026-10-15 14:30) in the Note Inbox, opens it and starts transcribing. While it transcribes, the button turns into a Stop transcribing button and shows the time elapsed.
  4. To end the transcription, press the Stop transcribing button. Trilium finishes transcribing what was already said before it stops.

Only one meeting can be transcribed at a time. The note itself is an ordinary text note: you can take notes in it during the meeting, rename it or move it anywhere in the tree. It carries the #meetingRecording label.

If no audio arrives from your microphone or from the meeting for 10 seconds, Trilium shows a message, the time on the button turns red and the button's tooltip says that no audio is arriving. Silence does not cause this: a muted microphone still delivers audio. Stop the transcription and start it again; what was transcribed until then is kept.

If the transcription stops on its own, for example because the audio could no longer be captured, Trilium shows a message and keeps everything transcribed until then. Closing Trilium during a meeting waits for the transcription to finish first.

The transcript

The transcript is stored as an attachment of the meeting note and is shown in a collapsible Transcript section above the note's content. It updates as the meeting goes on, one paragraph per speaker turn, each with the time it started. While the meeting is being transcribed, the header of the Transcript section shows the level of your microphone (Me) and of the meeting's audio (Others), so you can tell that both are being heard, even while the section is collapsed; a source that delivers no audio is marked No audio. While someone is talking, Speaking… shows under their name until their words are transcribed. If you are reading the end of the transcript, it scrolls to follow new text.

  • What you say into your microphone is labeled Me.
  • What the other participants say is labeled Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on, while Identify speakers is on; otherwise it is labeled Others.
  • To name a speaker, click their name, type the new name and press Enter. The name changes on every turn of that speaker. Press Esc to cancel, or clear the name to restore the default one.
  • To show the transcript with or without hesitations such as "uh", "um" and stuttered words, use the Hide filler words switch above it. This affects only what is shown; the stored transcript keeps every word.

If the meeting note is protected and the protected session is locked during the meeting, the transcript cannot be saved. Trilium keeps it in memory and saves it once you enter the protected session again, as long as Trilium stays open.

Configuration

The settings are in Options → Transcription, and are shown once Meeting transcription is on.

Before turning on Keep recordings for troubleshooting, ask everyone in the meeting to agree to being recorded. In several countries and US states, recording a conversation without everyone's consent is a criminal offense. The audio is stored unencrypted, even for protected notes. While the option is on, the tooltip of the Transcribe meeting button says so.

Limitations

  • Meeting transcription needs a speech recognition engine that does not load on every computer, for example on Linux distributions older than Ubuntu 22.04 or Debian 12. Where it does not load, the Transcribe meeting button does not appear, the Transcription options say so, and the log records why.
  • The transcript is made from the audio played by your device. A participant whose voice does not come out of your speakers or headphones, for example someone in the same room, is transcribed only as far as your microphone picks them up, and is labeled Me.
  • Speaker identification works best with a few clearly distinct voices. Similar voices, many participants or poor call quality can lead to speakers being merged or split.
  • Accuracy is lower for languages other than English, and the model can switch to the wrong language for short passages.
  • Transcribing uses a noticeable amount of processor time and memory, especially with the High accuracy model.
